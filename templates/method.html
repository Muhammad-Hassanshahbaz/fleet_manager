Method & concepts

How the system turns raw refuelling records into information.

1. Fuel economy: full-tank-to-full-tank method

  1. All records of a vehicle are sorted by date/time.
  2. A full-tank fill is a baseline: we know the tank is full at that odometer reading.
  3. At the next full-tank fill, the fuel burned since the baseline equals all litres added after the baseline, including any partial top-ups.
  4. km/L = (odometer now − odometer at baseline) ÷ litres added since baseline
    L/100 km = 100 ÷ km/L
    cost per km = money paid since baseline ÷ km since baseline
  5. The first record of a vehicle has no previous odometer reading, so it is only a baseline and never shows an economy figure.
  6. Partial fills show distance travelled, but their economy is counted at the next full tank.
  7. A record with an odometer problem (lower or equal reading, confirmed by the user) is excluded and starts a fresh baseline, so one bad reading cannot distort results.
Example: Full fill at 10,000 km → partial 15 L at 10,250 km → full 22 L at 10,480 km. Economy = (10,480 − 10,000) ÷ (15 + 22) = 480 ÷ 37 = 12.97 km/L.

3. Dashboard definitions

  • Total km served: sum of distance between consecutive odometer readings (records dated in the filter period).
  • Total fuel consumed / expenditure: sum of litres / receipt amounts in the period.
  • Overall cost/km and fleet km/L: computed from valid full-to-full intervals only, as total ÷ total (a weighted average), not as an average of averages.
  • Most / least economical: highest / lowest km/L among vehicles with at least one valid interval.
  • Highest fuel consumer: most litres in the period. This is a total, so a large truck can be the highest consumer and still be efficient for its size.
  • Anomaly: an interval below {{ (threshold*100)|int }}% of the vehicle's median km/L (needs at least 4 intervals). Possible fuel theft, a leak, a mechanical fault or a wrong entry.
  • History is always calculated on all records, then filtered by date, so an interval that starts before the filter period is still correct.

4. Concepts

Raw data vs information
Raw data is what was entered: odometer, litres, price. Information is what a manager can act on, e.g. "the LES-3021 truck costs more than twice as much per km as ISB-7788".
Calculated fields
Total cost, distance, km/L, L/100km and cost/km are worked out from raw fields and not stored, so they always match the data (shaded columns on the Refuelling page).
Database records
Three related tables: vehicles, refuels and expenses, linked by vehicle_id (one vehicle has many refuels and expenses).
Time-series trend
Values grouped by month and plotted in order, showing whether costs are rising, seasonal or affected by price changes.
Data validation
Checks at entry time that stop impossible or suspicious records from corrupting the results (section 2).
Aggregation
Combining many records into one number: SUM of litres, COUNT of fills, GROUP BY vehicle or month.
Average vs total
A total measures scale (who uses the most fuel). An average measures efficiency (who uses fuel best). Fleet km/L is a weighted average (total km ÷ total litres), so long-distance vehicles count more.
Manager decisions
Move routes to efficient vehicles, service or replace the least economical ones, investigate anomalies, budget from the monthly trend, compare cost/km before buying a new vehicle, and schedule services from the reminders.
